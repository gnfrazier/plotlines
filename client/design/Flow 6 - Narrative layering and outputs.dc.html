Linn Cove monument
ON ARRIVAL · 120 M
WHAT THE GROUP READS ON ARRIVAL

The viaduct you just rode under took eight years and cost more than every other mile of the parkway combined. The rock underneath it is Precambrian — older than anything with a spine.

The engineers gave up on cut-and-fill and built it in segments from above, so the mountain never carried a road crew. Look at the piers: no two are the same height.

Fill your bottles at the lot before you climb out. The next water is Bakersville, twenty-two miles on.

Day 2 — Old Fort to Linville Falls
WED 23 SEP · 51.8 MI · ↑ 4,120 FT · 4:45 MOVING
PLOTLINES
PAGE 1 OF 2

Out of Old Fort on the river road, then up. The Parkway carries you the last eleven miles, and the day ends where the water does.

0.0
S
Old Fort depot. Water and toilets at the trailside park; the last flush before the climb. The depot's story is on the board by the tracks — worth two minutes.
4.2
L
Onto Mill Creek Rd — gravel begins. Grade eases after the second cattle guard.
9.2
◆
Andrews Geyser. Spigot on the north side of the loop, potable. Shade and a bench. Regroup here — the next nine miles are the hard part.
18.6
↑
Rocky Fork begins. Eleven switchbacks, 6% average. Nobody has ever regretted going slower here.
38.4
Washout at CR-9 — dismount and carry left. The right shoulder drops four feet with no warning. Front light on if you are here after six.
40.9
★
Linn Cove monument. Restrooms, drinking fountains, café until five and closed Mondays. Fill everything — the next water is twenty-two miles on. There is something here to read when you arrive.
51.8
E
Linville Falls. Camp, water, and the falls a short walk from the sites. The day is done.
