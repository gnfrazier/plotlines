Your journey,
your story.

Plotlines is where an Author curates an adventure and writes it as a journey others read and live. Story here is structure, not decoration — it decides what objects exist, what the Author does, and what a Character receives and when. This guide defines how the brand looks, sounds, and behaves.

01

The Story

"Planning a journey is much like planning to write a story. You have your characters, your setting, and a plot — a route to follow."

Plotlines enables multi-modal adventure trips — cycling, hiking, paddling, cross-country skiing, climbing, packrafting — and stitches together the routes, cue sheets, transit legs, and points of interest that get a group from here to there. Getting there is part of the fun; the last mile to the trailhead is often the most memorable moment of the day.

Curation is the senior partner. An Author reads the land, judges which places are worth reaching, and routes to them — the route is downstream of an editorial decision, not upstream of it. The logistics stay rigorous underneath, but the narrative is how the whole thing is organized: it determines what objects exist, what an Author does, and what a Character receives, and when.

Positioning

The tool for authoring an adventure — curating what's worth reaching, routing between, and deciding what's known and what's discovered.

The Author
The creator

Reads the land, judges what's worth reaching, and promotes it into the trip. An editor as much as a planner — routing between chosen places and deciding what's revealed and what's known in advance.

The Character
The adventurer

Acts within the story in the field — arriving at plot points, choosing between authored branches, deciding whether to take on a station. Personalizes within the Author's bounds, and chronicles the day along the way.

Brand values
The filter for every requirement — a feature that serves none is a candidate for cutting, not adding.
01

Authorship over configuration

The Author is a creator, not a form-filler. Planning should feel like composing a journey; the strongest thing we build takes work off the Author — never adds another dial.

02

Curation over parity

We don't match every logistics feature of existing tools. We curate — places, themes, the shape of a day — and hand off the rest through clean export.

03

The journey is a story

Story is structure, not decoration — it decides what objects exist, what an Author does, and what a Character receives and when. Swap Author for "planner" and plot point for "waypoint"; if nothing breaks, the story has decayed back into vocabulary.

04

Quiet in the field

In-field software stays calm: works offline without nagging, degrades gracefully, respects the battery, and never interrupts a ride with a modal.

05

Portable & vendor-neutral

A Character's device, head unit, or platform is their choice. Plotlines exports cleanly and never traps a journey inside itself.

06

Honest state

The app tells the truth about what it knows — forecast freshness, sync, what's downloaded, what's still loading — plainly and without alarm.

07

Organized & logical

Structure is coherent and predictable. Segments sequence sensibly, data has one clear home, and decisions carry their rationale — everyone always knows what comes next.

08New

Reveal with intent

Every piece of content either reduces anxiety or sparks curiosity, and the Author decides which. Practical content is always visible; narrative may be held for arrival; safety content is never hidden by anyone, for any reason.

09New

No dice

We borrow the shape of a story, not the mechanics of a game. No points, achievements, unlockables, or randomness. Every route and every reveal is authored and deterministic.

02

Voice & Tone

The vocabulary — Author, Character, Plot Point, Arc, Set — names real objects in the product, not a costume laid over it. Use it plainly and consistently everywhere. Warmth rises and falls by context — fullest in marketing and the reading experience, quietest on the cue sheet — but the words are never swapped out for "planner" or "waypoint" to sound more technical. Clarity wins in the field; the story frame is how the product is organized, not a register you drop.

We say
  • "Ready to plot your next one?"
  • "Day 2 has 1,240 ft of climbing across 3 segments."
  • "Regroup at the clock tower — water and toilets on site."
  • "Add a plot point to mark the crux of the day."
  • "Water shows now; the overlook waits until you round the bend."
We don't say
  • "Begin your epic saga, brave hero!"
  • "Roughly a decent amount of uphill, probably."
  • "Meet up somewhere in the middle-ish."
  • "Insert dramatic narrative beat #4."
  • "Unlock the hidden waterfall — +50 XP!"

The Author voice

Confident, precise, generous with expertise — a trusted guide with a point of view. The Author is a creator, so the voice respects craft over configuration: it never talks down to a domain expert and never dresses a decision up as a quest. Plain when the moment is practical, warm when it's narrative.

The Character voice

Curious and observant — a participant, not a spectator. The Character acts within the story: arrives, chooses a branch, takes on or skips a station, and records the day. We invite, never assign — choices and journaling are offered, never issued as quests or scored.

Brand value 08 · Reveal with intent

Every line either calms or delights, and the Author decides which. Practical content speaks up front — water, bail-outs, shelter — in plain, reassuring language. Narrative content can hold its tongue until arrival, so rounding the bend is the reveal. Safety content is never hidden by anyone, for any reason — a surprise strainer is not a story beat.

Brand value 09 · No dice

We borrow the shape of a story, not the mechanics of a game. No points, badges, streaks, unlockables, or "you earned it" — nothing random, nothing gamified. The reward is the place and the day, described honestly. Every route and every reveal is authored and deterministic, and the voice never pretends otherwise.

The Frodo principle

"There is no point in the story where Frodo asks Samwise to wait up a minute."

Provisions live in the same voice as the story, never broken out into a logistics panel beside it. Water, toilets, and shelter are woven into the narrative register on itineraries, cue sheets, node cards, and print. Read a rendered day aloud: if the water source and the waterfall sound like two different documents, the voice has failed.

06

Iconography

Two families, drawn in the tradition of national-park and topographic cartography (NPS Harpers Ferry, USGS 7.5-minute, German Freizeitkarte). Mode icons (how you travel) are recognizable line pictographs on a 24px grid, 2px stroke, rounded caps — each a distinct silhouette. Node markers (what a place is) follow the topo rule that shape plus an internal mark carries the meaning, with color only reinforcing — so every marker still parses in one ink or in glare.

Circles = points on the route · squares = stops & transitions · triangle = warning. Internal mark disambiguates within a family.

08

Map & Data-Viz

Routes read like a topo sheet: color names the mode, the dash pattern names the surface or difficulty (after the German Freizeitkarte hiking tiers), and a casing under the line marks the selected leg (the USGS road-casing convention). Three independent layers, so a multi-modal day is legible without a key. Charts stay quiet — thin rules, mono labels, one accent per series.

Non-negotiables
  • 4.5:1 text contrast minimum; 3:1 for large display
  • Meaning never carried by color alone — pair with shape or label
  • 44px minimum touch targets in the field app
  • Miles/kilometers and °F/°C honored everywhere at once
  • Light, dark, and system themes all fully supported
10

Do & Don't

Do
  • Lead with function; earn the whimsy
  • Keep data in mono so numbers feel accountable
  • Use the story vocabulary in marketing and journaling
  • Let Authors work plainly if they prefer a spreadsheet feel
  • Invite Characters to chronicle — weather, discoveries, the day
  • Design for glare, gloves, and no signal
Don't
  • Force fantasy language into functional UI
  • Let the metaphor obscure a metric or a cue
  • Stack more than two accent colors in one view
  • Use color as the only signal for a node or route
  • Ship default screens in high-contrast mode
  • Round or fudge distances, elevation, or ETAs
