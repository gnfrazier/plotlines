# Plotlines — Research Spikes

**Purpose:** Feasibility, cost, and platform-behavior unknowns that should be de-risked with a time-boxed spike *before* the corresponding feature is committed to build. These are distinct from the Design decisions in the PRD's Open Items (which concern *what* to build); a spike answers *whether — and at what cost —* it can be built as written.

**How to read the priority:** **Scope-shaping** spikes can send you back to revise the PRD if they come back negative. **Implementation-informing** spikes won't change scope but determine how a committed feature is built. Do the scope-shaping ones first.

**Companion to:** `Plotlines_PRD.md` (103 FRs / 102 stories).

**Update (2026-08-16): SPIKE-21 ran and closed, and with it the desktop MVP has no unrun spike in front of it.** The cue-derivation algorithm F1 needed is implemented in `plotlines-core/trips/cues.py` (**ARCH D31**) and measured on nine routes over the three shared graphs. **Update (2026-08-28, later): SPIKE-C ran and produced the sharpest example yet of why this register exists.** SPIKE-C was *created* because a closed spike's verdict — SPIKE-04's, on `whitewater:section_grade` — had been generalised in v1.0 to every technical-terrain schema without being tested on any of them. Measured, the generalisation is wrong in both directions at once: **`piste:difficulty` is dense enough to read everywhere** (86.4–100%, and North American nordic *density* within 1.5–2.2× of the Tyrol's), while the three land schemas fail even in the Alpine valleys that invented them. **The half nobody predicted is the failure mode.** SPIKE-21 established that thin tagging produces *silence* — its unknown-tag rule made Davis emit no surface cues at all — and that precedent does not transfer, for a structural reason: cue derivation is per-edge, but a leg's difficulty is **worst-of** its ways, and a worst-of over a sample is biased low by construction. At real North American coverage **16–32% of the grades this feature would print are wrong, and every one of them is too easy.** So FR14b narrowed from "read it where it is published" to three clauses split by schema, and **Author declaration became the *primary* source for land difficulty rather than the fallback** — an inversion of the requirement's own wording. Two spikes now (SPIKE-D, SPIKE-C) have come back saying the conclusion survives but the stated reason does not; that is worth treating as the register's characteristic result rather than a coincidence.

**Update (2026-08-30): SPIKE-F ran — the last of the lettered series bar SPIKE-E, and the only one that is a decision rather than a measurement.** SPIKE-F is explicitly *"a joint design, security and product call, not a library choice"*, so its harness exists to make each answer checkable, not to discover a cost. Logs for an accountless reader are two-tier: edge/CDN ≤72 h, application logs through a field allowlist ≤30 d. **Recorded as ARCH D59; Q17 closed, A26 amended; PRD FR138/FR116, story H13's gate, and two §10 open items updated.** No product code changed — same discipline as SPIKE-A/C/D/G/H. ## Packaging & distribution (desktop MVP foundation)

### SPIKE-00 — Frozen sidecar packaging ✅ **COMPLETE**
**Covers:** ARCH §4 (portability), risks A1/A5, Open Questions Q4/Q5 — the foundation of desktop MVP
**Priority:** Scope-shaping — **was the only spike blocking the near-term build**
**Run:** 2026-08-13 (Linux) + 2026-08-14 (Windows) · **Result:** [`spikes/SPIKE-00/results/RESULTS.md`](../spikes/SPIKE-00/results/RESULTS.md) and [`WINDOWS.md`](../spikes/SPIKE-00/results/WINDOWS.md) — **the sidecar model holds (ARCH D1 confirmed).** Q4 → PyInstaller `--onedir`; Q5 → bundle in the installer. Identical routes on both platforms; §7.3's stop contract corrected for Windows. **Closed.**
**Unknown:** Can `plotlines-core` plus its heavy native dependency tree (GDAL, GEOS, rasterio, numpy, shapely) actually be frozen into a standalone binary that the Flutter app spawns as a child process, serves FastAPI on loopback, and shuts down cleanly — at an acceptable binary size and cold-start time? Every other spike concerns a later milestone; this one gates the thing being built first.
**Spike question:** On your primary desktop platform, freeze `service`+`core` (try PyInstaller and Nuitka — Q4) into a single binary; confirm it spawns, answers a `/health` and a real `/segments/generate` call over loopback, and terminates on signal. Measure binary size and cold-start-to-ready time. Repeat on a second desktop OS to expose cross-platform surprises. Assess bundle-in-installer vs. download-on-first-run (Q5) from the resulting size.
**Decides:** Whether the sidecar model (ARCH D1) holds for desktop as designed, and the answers to Q4 (which freezer) and Q5 (bundle vs. download). A negative result reshapes the entire desktop delivery approach before any UI is built.
**Done when:** A frozen binary generates a real route over loopback on two desktop platforms, with size and startup time quantified — or the blocking failure is documented and the delivery model revisited.

### SPIKE-14 — Vector mapping: `maplibre_gl` + PMTiles ✅ **COMPLETE**
**Covers:** ARCH §7.2 (`GET /tiles/{z}/{x}/{y}`), §9.2 (tile storage per platform), §11 (which had **no tile-provider row**), §11.3 (offline package size), FR35/FR64 (buffer + offline package, later); MVP §1.4.5 — the first of the four "decisions this list cannot make for itself"
**Priority:** **Gated desktop MVP**
**Run:** 2026-08-15 · **Result:** [`spikes/SPIKE-14/results/RESULTS.md`](../spikes/SPIKE-14/results/RESULTS.md) — **negative on the named hypothesis, positive on the goal.** `maplibre_gl` 0.26.2 supports **android, ios, web only**; there is nothing to test on desktop. Its successor `maplibre` 0.3.5 is tagged `windows, linux, macos` on pub.dev — and those tags are **wrong**: its plugin manifest declares only three implementations, and a Linux build compiles, links, opens a window, and then throws `UnsupportedError: MapLibre is not supported on this platform` at widget construction. A technology note written from pub.dev tags would have put a map on every Author Desktop screen before discovering this. **The GPU-native MapLibre path does not exist on Flutter desktop.**
**What does work:** `flutter_map` 8.3.1 + `vector_map_tiles` 9.0.0-beta.11 + `vector_tile_renderer` 6.1.0, which rasterizes vector tiles onto Flutter's own canvas. It rendered a real 6,864-vertex routed polyline with 60 node markers over a correct Protomaps v4 basemap **inside a network namespace with no route and no DNS** (`unshare -rn`) — P2's offline claim, verified at the OS level rather than by asking the app.
**Performance (Linux pass — software rasterization, `llvmpipe`, no GPU; every figure is a floor, and see the Windows/GPU numbers below):** **route geometry is free** — 0% of frames over the 16.7 ms budget at 1.2k, 6.9k *and* 41k vertices; the 41k case is ~660 km of OSM-noded route, well past any real trip. **The basemap is the entire cost, and it is a first-view cost**: cold, 14–47% of frames miss budget while tiles decode; warm, p95 falls to **15.5 ms** and jank to **2%**. Offline costs nothing measurable (73 vs 77 fps). Memory is the number to watch — the basemap adds ~400 MB RSS (~280 → ~680 MB).
**Three findings that change the build:** (1) the last **stable** `vector_map_tiles` (8.0.0, Aug 2024) pins `flutter_map ^7` and a renderer that **silently drops every road** from the current Protomaps v4 basemap — no error, just an unusable map; the working combination is a **pre-release**. (2) **No labels render on any renderer version** — the v4 themes drive labels through `format`/`case`/`is-supported-script` expressions the Dart renderer does not implement (**since fixed in the style — see Labels below**). (3) **PMTiles cannot be read inside the Flutter client**: `pmtiles` 1.x/2.x conflict with the modern `vector_map_tiles` on `latlong2` and `protobuf` respectively, and the bridge package pins the broken stable. No version triple resolves.
**Settled:** The basemap stack (`flutter_map`, not MapLibre — **ARCH D22**), the tile source and licence (Protomaps Basemap, **ODbL**, OSM attribution required, hotlinking discouraged so we mirror — **PRD FR95, ARCH D23**, filling the missing §11 tile row), and **the sidecar-vs-client fork, in the sidecar's favour** — not by preference but because the client-side path is dependency-blocked, which independently vindicates FR92/D21. Also answers **Q9** (extract from the published planet build in ~6 s rather than generate tiles — Planetiler stays on the shelf for custom layers) and most of **Q10** (~3.5 MB per 1,000 km² at z0–15; 1.0 MB CI bbox, 22 MB for an 80 km square, 118 MB for a multi-day corridor; z14 halves it).
**Second platform — run 2026-08-15, closing the clause the first pass could not reach:** the harness builds and renders on **Windows 11 with no source change** (`flutter create --platforms=windows .`; the only edit was a non-Linux fallback for the harness's own `/proc/self/status` RSS probe, which had been silently returning zero). Same 8-cell matrix, **byte-identical inputs** — same route payloads, same 171 tiles, verified by hash. On real GPU hardware (Intel Iris Xe) frames are **2–3× faster in the median** (basemap multi cold 13.1 → 5.5 ms; warm p95 15.5 → **7.6 ms**, 1% over budget) — and **no faster in the tail** (cold p99 45.5 → 64.8 ms, *worse*). Two rasterizers, same shape: **the cost that survives hardware acceleration is tile decode, not drawing**, which is where any future optimization belongs. Route geometry is free on both. **Memory went the other way and is the number to carry: ~1.0–1.2 GB working set on Windows against ~680 MB RSS on Linux** — different metrics, not subtractable, but the client should be budgeted near 1 GB. Offline was re-verified more weakly there (socket audit by PID — no remote endpoint observed; a firewall rule needs Administrator), so **the Linux `unshare -rn` result stays the load-bearing evidence for P2**.
**Labels — fixed, in the style rather than the renderer (2026-08-15).** The gap turned out not to be missing label support but **two specific expression constructs a style can avoid**: `text-field`'s multi-script name fallback (`format`/`coalesce`/`is-supported-script`) on 10 symbol layers, and the expression form of `in` in one filter. `probes/simplify_labels.py` rewrites both, and street, path and waterway names render with halos and line placement ([`shots/windows-labels-z15.png`](../spikes/SPIKE-14/results/shots/windows-labels-z15.png)) at no measurable frame cost. **Recommendation: ship a Plotlines-authored theme generated by that transform, in the tile pipeline beside the mirror** — not a renderer fork and not an upstream contribution. Two limits recorded: `["get","name"]` is local-name-only (fine for the US/English MVP, a debt for later locales), and the `pois` layer still needs a per-feature zoom threshold replaced by a static one — a cartographic decision, not a rewrite, and park/peak/beach names are what it costs until it is made.
**Left open, deliberately:** **macOS**, which was never claimed — two desktop platforms was the bar and two is what there is. The **pre-release dependency** is unchanged: the working stack is a `vector_map_tiles` beta, and whether to ship on it or wait for 9 stable is a call this spike surfaces rather than makes (ARCH A14). **Closed.**
**Already decided, out of this spike's scope (2026-08-15, from the cycling-tour-planner POC — PRD FR92–94, ARCH D21):** the client talks only to Plotlines' own `GET /tiles/{z}/{x}/{y}` service, never a third-party tile host directly; the service validates `z/x/y` range before any upstream work; tile generation/caching is bbox-scoped and on-demand (not a standing global server), sharing one pipeline with offline bundles. **Unknown:** Every screen in the Author Desktop wireframe is a map, and **no document in this repo names a basemap.** The integration table in ARCH §11 lists elevation, weather, geocoding, OSM Overpass, and USGS — and no tiles. `client/pubspec.yaml` declares no dependencies at all. ### SPIKE-21 — Cue derivation from a routed polyline ✅ **COMPLETE**
**Run:** 2026-08-16 · **Result:** [`spikes/SPIKE-21/results/RESULTS.md`](../spikes/SPIKE-21/results/RESULTS.md) — **positive; the algorithm is implemented in `plotlines-core/trips/cues.py` (ARCH D31)** and every fixture route lands inside the legibility ceiling. Nine routes over the three shared graphs — 132.3 km, 5,235 polyline vertices, 1,314 junctions — reduce to **367 cues**, of which 266 are derived turns. Derived density is **0.87–2.86 cues/km** against a ceiling of 4.0 declared before the run, and no kilometre anywhere carries more than 8 cues of any kind.
**The first implementation failed that ceiling** (4.2–7.3 cues/km in Boulder and Davis; four cues inside twenty metres at one path/road interchange), and what fixed it was not threshold tuning:
1. **Staying on the same way is not a turn** — a named greenway that swings 60° at a bridge and keeps its name is "continue". Removed **199 of the 509** candidates surviving the 30° test, the single largest reduction.
2. **Turns inside 40 m are one manoeuvre** — a staggered crossing is one decision, not three. Removed a further 31.
**The threshold sweep is the counter-evidence:** across smoothing window (10/25/50 m) × straight-through angle (20/30/45°), Boulder's via-loop turn count moves only 30→39 and Viroqua's not at all (27→28). The parameters an implementer tunes first were never where the leverage was.
**Three findings with build consequences:**
1. **Density must be judged on the *derived* half.** Author content — hazards, portage, transition, alternate, nodes, start/finish — is a fixed ~11 cues that do not scale with distance, so a 3.9 km route is 2.8 cues/km before a single turn is derived. The derivation may not thin an Author's hazard to hit a density target, and `derive_cue_sheet` reports the two densities separately.
2. **Surface cues are coverage-bound, and that narrows F1.** Six surface cues in 132 km: Boulder (81.7% tagged) emitted 4, **Davis (34.4%) emitted none on any route**, and **Viroqua (24.5%) — genuinely gravel country — emitted 2**. The unknown-tag rule works exactly as designed, and what it produces on thin tagging is silence. F1's "including surface shifts" is best-effort, never inferred from road class (**ARCH A17**'s caution), and FR4's *singletrack* class has no source in `surface` at all.
3. **The removed turn weight surfaces here first.** ARCH **D6** deliberately dropped "fewest turns" from the scorer, which is invisible until a cue sheet exists — and then it is a Boulder loop with a derived turn every 360 m. Not an argument to reverse D6; an argument that cues-per-km belongs on D1's dashboard, now that derivation costs 1.1–9.2 ms per route.
**SPIKE-20's seam held as advertised:** the spike needed exactly one new field, `cue.retrace`, and it cost a schema patch (payload schema 1.0.0 → **1.1.0**, additive). Verified across the client boundary rather than asserted — the Boulder payload through SPIKE-20's Dart harness returned **139 of 139 cues with all 8 retrace flags intact**, byte-identical.
**Left open, deliberately:** no paddling or hiking route was cued (all nine are on the cycling graph; water cue sheets are authored content plus nothing derived); no scheduled-event conflict path (FR28's timeline half is P1); instructions are English strings built in the core, which FR83 will want as structured data; and no grade/elevation cue exists, which is a product call this spike did not take. **Closed.**
**Covers:** PRD F1 `[MVP]` (per-day cue sheets, FR46), FR20 (alternates tagged on cue sheets), FR27 (hazard/crux warnings on cue sheets), FR15 (portages auto-included), FR28 (scheduled events populate cue sheets); ARCH §5.2/§5.3 (the Field Runtime's cue-*consumption* engine, which assumes a precomputed cue list already exists on-device — this spike is what produces it, a distinct question from how it's *tracked* in the field); depends on **SPIKE-20** for where derived output is written
**Priority:** Gates desktop MVP — F1 is `[MVP]` and no document specifies the algorithm
**Unknown:** A routed polyline out of `plotlines-core` is an OSMnx graph path — noded at every OSM vertex, not at real intersections; SPIKE-14 measured **41k vertices on a single ~660 km route**. ## Routing-algorithm feasibility

### SPIKE-01 — Via-node loop routing ✅ **COMPLETE**
**Covers:** FR8a (Story A9)
**Priority:** Scope-shaping
**Run:** 2026-08-14 · **Result:** [`spikes/SPIKE-01/results/RESULTS.md`](../spikes/SPIKE-01/results/RESULTS.md) — **A9's own promotion condition is met literally.** Point-to-point, out-and-back, loop, and via-loop are all one call to `solve_circuit(graph, anchors, close=)` with a different anchor list; there is no via-node code path. Out-and-back and a 1-via loop are *the same anchor list*. The cost is not merely acceptable but **negative**: a 1-via loop solves in **48 ms against the unconstrained loop's 295 ms**, because a via *replaces* a synthesised shaping anchor the engine would otherwise have to place and tune. Weights are still honoured — mean edge cost per metre stays within **0.976–1.014** of the same theme's unconstrained loop — and 24/24 runs hit every via and closed.
**Limit found:** at **three or more via-nodes the target distance stops being honourable** — error jumps from under ±14% to **+30.7% (Boulder) and +81.9% (Viroqua)** — because the vias themselves determine the loop's length and leave the distance search nothing to move. This is exactly the case A9 hands to A6, and that hand-off was verified working (the via-node is named as the binding constraint, not the terrain).
**Also settled — degenerate routes:** retracing is fixed by *where* the re-ride penalty applies, not how large it is. A flat penalty cannot distinguish "don't ride the corridor back" from "you may ride the café's dead-end lane back". A locality-aware penalty (full charge in the corridor, near-neutral inside a ball of 5% of target distance around Author-designated points) cut corridor doubling from **41.7% to 6.0%** while *improving* distance conformance — the "lollipop": a spur ridden twice, hung off a loop ridden once.
**Scope decision taken (2026-08-14, PRD FR8a):** the story was **split**. **A9** (one or two via-nodes) promoted from P1 to **MVP** and can be built and closed on its own; **A9a** (three or more) is a new **P1** backlog story where target distance is presented as advisory and A6's relaxation path is offered in the same interaction. **Closed.**
**Unknown:** Can the OSMnx/solver approach constrain a loop to pass through one or more mandatory via-nodes while still honoring weights and a target distance — without unacceptable compute time or degenerate routes?
**Spike question:** Generate loops through 1, 2, and 3 forced via-nodes on a real graph; measure solve time and route quality vs. an unconstrained themed loop.
**Decides:** Whether A9 is promotable to MVP. If via-node and start/destination turn out to be the same constraint primitive, A9 is nearly free and should land in MVP; if not, it stays P1.
**Done when:** A via-node loop generates in acceptable time and returns a sensible route, or the cost is quantified and the P1/MVP call is made on evidence.

### SPIKE-02 — Conflict detection & relaxation ✅ **COMPLETE**
**Covers:** FR9 (Story A6)
**Priority:** Implementation-informing
**Run:** 2026-08-14 · **Result:** [`spikes/SPIKE-02/results/RESULTS.md`](../spikes/SPIKE-02/results/RESULTS.md) — **A6's acceptance criteria are achievable as written; no adjustment to them is needed.** Across 8 scenarios: **8/8 classified correctly, 8/8 named exactly the right constraints, 0 false conflicts** on satisfiable controls, and **5/5 offered relaxations were applied and verified to actually route**. The deletion filter correctly narrowed a three-band conflict to the two bands that actually bind, dropping the one that did not.
**Key distinction the spike forced:** "name the conflicting constraints" splits in two, and collapsing them produces confidently wrong advice. **Unattainable alone** — the band is outside what the graph can produce at any weights ("nothing from here climbs 300 m in 20 km"; a measurement, and nothing else is at fault). **Conflicting in combination** — each is reachable alone but not together ("the climbing is up the busy road"; the useful offer is which *one* to loosen). Reporting the second as the first would tell an Author their mountain town is flat.
**Cost, which shapes the UI more than any other finding:** a satisfiable request is ~1 solve (**27–218 ms**), but diagnosis costs **1.3–15.0 s** and scales with the number of bands. **A6's explanation cannot be produced synchronously inside a route request** — return the best-effort route with its violations immediately, then stream the named conflict and relaxations.
**Honesty boundary:** the search underneath is incomplete, so a combination conflict is worded "no route was **found** meeting them together", never "impossible". Only the unattainable verdict rests on a measurement. **Closed.**
**Unknown:** Can the solver introspect an infeasible constraint set to name *which* constraints conflict and propose the nearest relaxation — rather than just returning an empty result?
**Spike question:** Construct several deliberately-infeasible weight/constraint combinations; determine whether the engine can identify the binding constraints and compute a minimal relaxation.
**Done when:** The engine names a real conflict and a valid relaxation for the test cases, or the limitation is documented so A6's AC can be adjusted.

### SPIKE-03 — Min/max weight-band convergence ✅ **COMPLETE**
**Covers:** FR6 (Story A5)
**Priority:** Implementation-informing
**Run:** 2026-08-14 · **Result:** [`spikes/SPIKE-03/results/RESULTS.md`](../spikes/SPIKE-03/results/RESULTS.md) — **bands converge fine; absolute band *defaults* are what over-constrain.** Same solver, same three graphs, same 20 km target: **8/36 (22.2%) feasible with fixed absolute defaults** (climbing 100–400 m × traffic ≤15/25/35%) versus **3/3 with defaults derived from each region's attainable envelope**. The 77.8% failure rate is manufactured entirely by asking for numbers the place cannot produce, and would have read as "min/max bands don't work" had the envelope not been measured separately. **Band sliders must open on the range the region can actually deliver** — probing costs 10 solves and can be cached per region and distance.
**Band behaviour characterised (the "done when"):** two-sided bands hold down to **±10% of the envelope centre everywhere and ±5% in two regions of three**; solve count is the early-warning signal, climbing toward the budget as a band approaches infeasibility. Precision should be floored in **absolute** units (≈25 m of climbing), not percentages — ±5% of Davis's 19 m is a ±0.95 m window no engine should promise. **Distance must be banded like any other metric**: left unbanded, the compromise silently spent up to **+14.8%** extra mileage to satisfy climbing and traffic.
**Requirements conflict found and resolved (2026-08-14, PRD FR6 / A5):** FR6 said Authors set a min/max on any **weight**; A5's AC says the engine "returns a route **within all bands** where one exists; where none exists, A6 governs." Both cannot hold. A band on a *weight value* can never be infeasible — any number inside the band is a legal weight — which makes A5's clause unreachable and A6 dead code. This spike therefore treats a band as an acceptance range on the **realised route attribute** ("between 400 and 600 m of climbing"), the only reading under which A5's own AC means anything — and **FR6 has been reworded to bound the attribute**, with A5's AC kept and extended to require envelope-derived defaults and an absolute precision floor. **Closed.**
**Two weight-shape limits found:** FR4's surface weight is **unipolar** and cannot *seek* gravel (only tolerate it), so no unpaved-minimum band is satisfiable anywhere — it needs a bipolar weight like FR2's `peaks`. And traffic stress inferred from **highway class alone** overstates rural traffic badly, giving rural Viroqua a 35% traffic *floor* on empty county roads.
**Unknown:** Does compromise-finding across multiple bounded (min/max) weights converge to a good route, or do bands routinely over-constrain into infeasibility?
**Spike question:** Run realistic competing bands (e.g. high climbing-min + low traffic-max) across varied geographies; measure how often a valid route exists and whether it's good.
**Done when:** Band behavior is characterized well enough to set sensible default ranges and know how often A6's conflict path will fire.

### SPIKE-C — Non-whitewater difficulty-grading coverage ✅ **COMPLETE**
**Covers:** PRD **FR14b**, FR14 — story **B9** `[P1]`; ARCH §7.4 (`terrain_technicality`), **Q13**
**Priority:** Implementation-informing, conditionally scope-shaping — a positive result *adds* a capability FR14 currently makes the Author supply by hand
**Run:** 2026-08-28 · **Result:** [`spikes/SPIKE-C/results/RESULTS.md`](../spikes/SPIKE-C/results/RESULTS.md) — **split, and the interesting half is not the coverage half.** Across **57,422 ways and 12,735 km in seven regions**, exactly **one of FR14b's four schemas clears the threshold declared before the run, and it clears it everywhere**: `piste:difficulty` at **100%** (Methow Valley), **86.4%** (White Mountains) and **97.2%** (Tyrol). The three land schemas clear nowhere, including at home — best-anywhere is `mtb:scale:imba` **39.3%** (Bentonville singletrack) and `sac_scale` **31.8%** (White Mountains, `route=hiking` members) against **55.1%** in the Tyrol, where the scale was invented.

**This spike exists because a closed spike's verdict was applied past the question it answered**, and the design is built around not repeating that. Three things were fixed before any query was issued. **Four denominators**, reported side by side widest to narrowest — what a routed leg is made of, `highway=path`, `route=*` membership, and (MTB) any prior `mtb:*` edit — because the denominator is the one number a coverage spike can quietly draft to taste, and the narrowing is the argument: the reader watches the figure fail to improve as each concession is made. **A region set that is three concentric controls rather than a sample** — the three shared fixtures, one *mode-popular* North American region per mode (White Mountains / Bentonville / Methow Valley, on the issue's own point that a niche schema tracks the local mapper community more than the terrain), and the **Tyrol as schema homeland**, without which a continent-wide zero is unreadable: it could mean the tag is dead, and it is not. **And a `read`/`opportunistic`/`absent` band declared in advance** (≥70% / ≥20% / below), SPIKE-21-style, with anything under 30 eligible ways reported `n/a` rather than `absent` — an unmeasurable cell and an empty one are different findings.

**The same-place controls are what make a zero readable, and they close the question.** On Bentonville's own 4,238 MTB-eligible ways — 239 `route=mtb` relations, a professionally built and signed network — `surface` is at **74.4%**, `name` at 59.7%, `width` at 32.2%, and `mtb:scale` at **0.76%**. These are among the best-attributed trail in the dataset. Nobody is waiting to get round to them; the community chose a different schema. That distinction — *unused* rather than *un-mapped* — is the difference between a fixable problem and a closed one, and it is only visible because the control tags were measured over literally the same ways from the same pull.

**The finding that changes the requirement is about failure mode, not coverage.** SPIKE-21's precedent said thin tagging produces *silence* — its unknown-tag rule meant Davis emitted no surface cues at all, a loss of capability and nothing worse. **That does not transfer, for a structural reason.** Cue derivation is per-edge, so an untagged edge costs one cue. A difficulty grade for a leg is **worst-of** its ways, and a worst-of taken over a sample is **biased low by construction** — every error the mechanism can produce is in the dangerous direction, and overstatement cannot occur at all. Measured on 63 fully-graded real Tyrolean trails (418 ways, 187 km) thinned to the rates North America actually has: **16–32% of the leg grades this feature would print are wrong, all of them too easy**, at a mean of 1.1 SAC steps. Interpolating for where that falls below the 10% ceiling declared in advance gives a harm-derived floor of **≈32–38%** — independent corroboration of the pre-declared 70% band, arrived at from the damage rather than from A19's precedent. Nothing in North America comes near either.

**Two further results reach past the question asked.** **The two MTB scales are regional mirror images**: `mtb:scale:imba` is 39.3% in Bentonville and **0.10%** in the Tyrol, while `mtb:scale` is 29.5% in the Tyrol and 0.19% in Bentonville. They are not two measurements of one schema's health; they are two regional schemas describing the same thing, so a single-schema implementation is wrong on one continent by construction — FR14b already listed both, which turns out to be right for a reason it did not state. And **North American nordic is not a thin version of the European case**: the Methow's 299 pistes at 182 per 1,000 km² and the Whites' 575 at 270 sit within 2.2× and 1.5× of the Tyrol's density. The coverage percentage there is self-selecting (the same mapper writes `piste:type` and `piste:difficulty` in one edit) and means little on its own; read beside the density it means the mode is genuinely there.

**The honesty clause shipped as a type, not a comment.** FR14b and B9 both require coverage stated honestly rather than sparse data presented as complete, and the issue asked for it "in a form the UI can actually surface beside the grade" (A19's precedent). `coverage.CoverageNote` carries three states and makes the sparse case *structurally* unable to render as the complete one — in the silent state there is no value to print even by accident. Real output: *"mountain_hiking is the hardest grade among 3 of 7 graded ways (9.6 of 20.5 km) — this is a floor, not a summary"*; *"No published sac_scale for this leg. 34 ways, 31.0 km, none graded — the grade is the Author's to declare, not the map's to supply."* Two values-level hazards came out of real data: **the vocabularies are not closed** (`sac_scale=strolling`, `=undefined`; `trail_visibility=very_bad`, `=poor`; `mtb:scale:imba=2.5`), reported as used-but-unparsed rather than counted as untagged, because a schema that is used but not machine-readable is a different finding from one that is unused; and `piste:difficulty`'s published vocabulary carries `freeride`/`extreme`, which are not ordinal continuations of `novice…expert` — none occur in this sample, but nordic is the schema that ships a real aggregate and it is the one place an off-scale value would silently sort wrong.

**Consequences, applied 2026-08-28:** **FR14b narrowed from one clause to three** — nordic reads and aggregates; hiking and MTB read **per way and never aggregate**; **Author declaration (FR14/B8) is the *primary* source for land leg difficulty, not the fallback**, which inverts v2.0's original wording. **B9's AC narrowed to the schemas that cleared**, per the issue's own "done when". **ARCH §7.4 rewritten** — `terrain_technicality` is no longer "unproven", it is measured and it stays Author-declared. **ARCH Q13 closed.** **No product code changed** — `coverage.py` is a spike-local prototype, same discipline as SPIKE-D and SPIKE-H.

**Left open, deliberately:** everything here is measured **against OSM**. FR14b says *"where a source does publish"*, and a plugin layer — a land manager's trail inventory, an IMBA-affiliated trail org's feed — could publish grading at coverage OSM does not have; SPIKE-H already merged real NPS data through `LayerProvider` cleanly. Nothing in this spike argues against that path, only that **OSM is not that source** for hiking and MTB, and `CoverageNote.source` exists for exactly that reason. Also unmeasured: whether `sac_scale` coverage is *growing* (this is one snapshot, not a trend), and the `block` thinning model orders ways by id as a proxy for editing session because geometry is discarded at fetch time — a weaker adjacency proxy than real topology would be.

**Unknown:** SPIKE-04 tested `whitewater:section_grade` specifically and found it effectively absent in North America — one graded feature across three regions. That verdict is correct and stands. **v1.0 then generalised it to all technical terrain without testing it**, and ARCH §7.4 said so out loud: *"`terrain_technicality` remains unproven, not proven."* This is the register's own failure mode showing up on the other side — a closed spike's verdict applied past the question it answered.
**Spike question:** Measure real coverage, per region, for each published difficulty schema OSM actually carries — `sac_scale`/`trail_visibility` (hiking), `mtb:scale`/`:uphill`/`:imba` (MTB), `piste:difficulty` (nordic) — over the shared fixture regions plus at least one region where the mode is genuinely popular. ### SPIKE-D — Layer extraction and POI indexing timing ✅ **COMPLETE**
**Covers:** PRD **FR121**, FR91 (amended), FR120 — stories **N1**, **N2**, **M12a** `[MVP]`; ARCH §8.3 (per-capability readiness, **breaking B1**), **D34**, risk **A23**
**Priority:** Gated the v2.0 authoring entry point — the punch-list marks it *"directly gates §1.1"*
**Run:** 2026-08-27/28 · **Result:** [`spikes/SPIKE-D/results/RESULTS.md`](../spikes/SPIKE-D/results/RESULTS.md) — **D34 is not confirmed; it is inverted.** On a real 704 km² trip bbox (PRD §5.4a's own worked-pass window, shared with SPIKE-B so the two compose), **layer extraction takes 15.8 s at best and 178.5 s at worst, while elevation enrichment takes 8.8 s.** Enrichment is **0.56×** extraction — the *shorter* half. FR121 puts the fast operation second and the slow, unpredictable one in front of the Author. And the capability that actually stays not-ready longest is neither: it is the **graph build**, at 36.7 s (704 km²) and 116.6 s (1,799 km²) — which the shipped sidecar already gates routing on correctly. **D34's prose disagrees with `service/`'s own code, not just with the measurement.**
**FR121's conclusion survives its premise.** Never blocking the app is still right; the reasoning has to change, because the honest wait at trip initiation is extraction, its length is *unpredictable rather than long*, and elevation is not what routing waits for.
**POI indexing does not exist as a cost.** `score_notability` is **2 ms** at trip scale and **51 ms** over 31,818 features at the largest extent FR120 permits (measured offline against SPIKE-B's committed pull, no query issued). "Layer extraction **and POI indexing**" names two costs with nothing in common and only one of them is real: **every second of FR121's first phase is network.**
**Risk A23 was right to worry and wrong about why.** Its claim that candidate extraction is *"a heavier query than graph building"* is **backwards** — a candidate pull is **×0.43 the time and ×0.78 the bytes** of a graph build over the same bbox, and the gap widens with area (×0.12 / ×0.24 at 1,799 km²). What is genuinely expensive is **variance: up to ×21 run-to-run on the identical query** (a 176 km² box took 8.8 s once and 185.9 s an hour later), all of it 429/504 throttling rather than query work. **Tiling is therefore a pessimisation below ~2,500 km²** — extraction scales *sub-linearly* (43 s/1,000 km² at 70 km² falling to 8 s/1,000 km² at 8,815 km²), tiled 2×2 was slower than whole-bbox in both runs, and osmnx already auto-splits above 2,500 km² without being asked. The load-bearing mitigations are **retry-with-backoff, bbox-scoped caching** (a warm re-read is **1.75 s vs 15.8 s**, the only measurement with no variance), and **multiple endpoints**.
**Two dependency defects the product inherits today**, both in `osmnx`, which `OsmLayerProvider.fetch` calls: its rate limiter **recurses forever** against an instance reporting no slot limit (it branches on line 4 of the status page; observed: 20 minutes asleep, no query sent), and its 429/504 retry has **no attempt limit** — a pull against a busy instance does not fail, it spins. So A23's "don't depend on one endpoint" **cannot be implemented as a URL swap**, and any failover list must be **coverage-checked rather than liveness-checked**: `overpass.osm.ch` answers 200 promptly and holds only Switzerland, so ranking by liveness would silently tell an Author their trip area is empty — the same class of silent substitution issue #154 was filed over on the routing side.
**The evening it ran is part of the evidence.** One uncached whole-extent multi-day pull ran **past 30 minutes without returning**, after which `overpass-api.de` **refused TCP from this host for the rest of the session**; two mirrors returned 500/502 and Geofabrik 502/503 throughout. A23 rates this **Medium**; a single spike's measurement run exhausted the primary public endpoint's tolerance.
**`/health`: the shipped app passes 3 of 8 clauses, a prototype passes 9 of 9.** Issue #154 built per-capability readiness and per-*region* routing readiness correctly, and B1 holds — a region settling to `failed:` leaves layers ready. What does not exist is the per-*layer* half **N2's AC requires and §8.3's own example prints**: `per_layer` is the constant `{layer: "ready" …}` with no seam at which a plugin layer could report `loading` or `failed:licence_missing`. Worse, **one bad layer takes the other five with it** — `GET /candidates` wraps one provider call in one `try` and returns **422 with zero candidates**, where the prototype returns 200 with the built-in candidates plus `layers_unavailable`. The root cause is the provider signature: `fetch` returns a bare `list[RawFeature]`, so "which layers did I actually get?" has nowhere to live and an exception is the only signal available. **That return-type change is the finding N2 has to build against**, not the state machine around it.
**Concurrency holds, and the progress estimate does not.** Enrichment does not starve extraction (notability scoring ×1.15, i.e. unaffected); the Author's surfaces take a bounded tax (`/health` p50 1.0 → 30.5 ms, p95 63.5 ms) that is real but imperceptible. The contention lands on **uvicorn's event loop**, not on computation — invisible to any benchmark that measures the computation. But **enrichment itself runs ×2.96 slower while the Author works**, so FR121's *"routing available in about 3 minutes"* will be wrong in the direction that matters and **wrong precisely when the Author is busiest**. `service/plotlines_service/app.py:55` already flags `GRAPH_ESTIMATED_S` as a heuristic awaiting SPIKE-D; the answer is that **a fixed constant cannot be honest**, and the indicator needs observed progress or a stated range.
**FR120/N1's incremental enlargement is exact and slower.** The added-area decomposition is correct twice over — offline partition of the cached pull and a live incremental run both give **873 = 873 candidates, 0 missing, 0 extra, no double-counted corner** — but re-extracting only the added area took **113.9 s over two queries against 13.9 s for one full re-extract**, for the same reasons as above. **N1's AC should be read as a correctness requirement, not a performance one**: the reasons not to refetch the whole bbox are that it wastes the commons and discards a cached result, not that it is faster.
**Left open, deliberately:** **local extracts** — A23's third mitigation is unmeasured (Geofabrik was down all session and no PBF tooling is in either project environment), and given the variance it is the one worth measuring next, since it removes the problem rather than working around it. Also: **GEDTM30 via OpenTopography**, since §1's enrichment figure is an AWS Terrarium stand-in at matched 30 m resolution (D20 forbids a second production source, and FR87/#148 gates the real one) — the inversion has ~4× headroom before it would reverse, but the 8.8 s should be re-measured when #148 lands; the **1,164 MB peak** on the 1,799 km² graph build against SPIKE-14's ~1 GB client budget; and `plugin_layers.LayerRegistry`, which is a demonstrated shape for N2 rather than a patch to `core/`. **Closed.**
**Unknown:** FR121 rewrites how the app starts, superseding FR91 v1.0's single readiness flag with per-capability readiness — layer extraction and POI indexing first, elevation lazily behind the Author. ARCH **D34** rests the whole thing on one unmeasured claim: *"Ordering is a reordering of existing startup work, not new work (SPIKE-D confirms)."* Nothing had confirmed it, and if extraction over a real trip bbox is itself minutes long, FR121 does not deliver what it promises — it just moves the spinner.
**Spike question:** On a realistic trip bbox in the frozen sidecar: time extraction-to-authorable against enrichment's own duration; confirm the two run concurrently without enrichment starving extraction and measure what the Author's surfaces feel like; exercise the `/health` contract including a slow and a failing plugin layer; measure what a candidate pull actually costs public Overpass (**A23**) and settle the access pattern; and confirm FR120's enlargement re-extracts only the added area.
**Decides:** Whether FR121's per-capability readiness delivers a usable authoring session during enrichment, or whether extraction itself needs to move — and confirms or breaks **D34**.
**Done when:** Both durations are measured on a real trip bbox, concurrency is proven, the per-capability/per-layer `/health` contract is exercised including a failing layer, and the Overpass access pattern is settled with numbers — **or the ordering claim in D34 is corrected on evidence. It was.**

### SPIKE-G — Candidate & proposal rendering at bbox scale ✅ **COMPLETE**
**Covers:** PRD **FR99**, FR102, FR105a, **FR108**, FR120 — stories **N3** `[MVP]`, **N4a** `[P1]`, **O3** `[MVP]`; ARCH **Q15** ★, risk **A16**
**Priority:** Implementation-informing, scope-shaping for the curation workspace's map half — run before N3
**Run:** 2026-08-29 · **Result:** [`spikes/SPIKE-G/results/RESULTS.md`](../spikes/SPIKE-G/results/RESULTS.md) — **salience-gating wins, and it is the only one of Q15's three options that satisfies FR99/N4a intent rather than just the frame budget.** SPIKE-14 proved route geometry free and the basemap ~1 GB but **measured no markers**; this spike prices a candidate layer over SPIKE-14's warm-frame anchors, at SPIKE-A's real post-calibration candidate densities in the `avl` / `lwr` / `sgv` trip bboxes (715 / 72 / **1,208** candidates, 79 / 20 / 100 of them polygon area anchors), reconstructed to real coordinates and rings from SPIKE-A's committed pulls.
**The naive layer — one hit-testable widget marker per candidate — is what SPIKE-14 did not test, and it breaks.** Warm basemap + candidates at the trip-overview zoom on a GPU desktop: `sgv` p95 **33.5 ms** against the 16.7 ms budget, `avl` 22.9 ms; on the software-raster floor 50–75 ms. It fits only in the sparse `lwr` (72). **Zoom-thresholding renders nothing below the threshold** — the overview, the one zoom the Author most needs to see the field co-location analysis runs over, is blank (hard fail, FR99/N3). **Clustering-for-display fits comfortably but aggregates salience into a count glyph and makes a map tap ambiguous** — it fails FR99's "salience visible" and N4a's "tap the cluster, select its card / promote from the map" without a zoom-in first. Good as the zoomed-out affordance and the over-ceiling backstop; wrong as the primary mode.
**Salience-gating: render the top-K in-viewport candidates by salience as hit-testable widget markers, the rest as a salience-styled canvas-dot field.** K is *derived*, not chosen — `(warm-frame headroom − 3 ms area/dot allowance) ÷ per-marker frame cost` ≈ **300 on a GPU desktop**, ~0 on the software-raster floor (a documented floor, not the target, exactly as SPIKE-14 framed its Linux numbers). Fits every zoom on GPU with headroom (`sgv` z10 15.8 ms), keeps salience visible *by construction* (the widgets **are** the notable ones), supports promote-from-map and exact tap→card. A canvas dot is ~50× cheaper per frame and ~180× cheaper in memory than a widget marker.
**Selection latency is a function of the live widget count, not of density.** The cost is not hit-testing — it is the `MarkerLayer` rebuild a naive `setState` triggers, re-laying-out every live marker on the tap: `sgv` z10 `map→list` is ~55 ms naive (1,207 widgets) against ~20 ms salience-gated (305). `list→map` is O(1) and density-independent (~20 ms) in every strategy. The issue's "300 ms to answer a tap is unusable" bar is never breached on latency — but clustering breaches it on *correctness*.
**Areas (FR108) are first-class and cheap on GPU (~1–2 ms/frame for every area anchor in a dense bbox), dominant on the software-raster floor** — ~100 filled+stroked polygons out-cost 1,200 point markers on `llvmpipe`. A GPU-less client would need to zoom-gate them; MVP desktop does not.
**Display-density ceiling: ~2,800 candidates in one viewport** on a GPU desktop before the p95 interaction frame tips. The shipped notability ruleset (SPIKE-A v1.2.0) stays well under it in a trip bbox (worst 1,208); the pre-calibration flood (`sgv` v1.1.0, **5,453**) is over it — so clustering is the backstop against a ruleset regression, not a routine mode.
**A16 re-measured with candidates on screen:** SPIKE-14's ~1 GB Windows basemap client **+ ~3 MB** for the salience-gated candidate layer at its density ceiling **+ ~150 MB** tile/transient headroom = **~1.15 GB**. **The candidate layer is not a material memory cost** under the recommended strategy (bounded widget count + flat dot/vertex arrays); A16's mitigation lever is unchanged — **tile-cache bounds**, not candidate culling. The ~1 GB basemap figure remains the number to re-measure on a release build.
**This is a recommendation on a model calibrated to SPIKE-14's measurements, not a fresh measurement** — the same standing as SPIKE-13's verdict. The load-bearing coefficient (per-frame widget-marker cost) is set from practitioner consensus; [`spikes/SPIKE-G/HARNESS.md`](../spikes/SPIKE-G/HARNESS.md) specifies the two-platform Flutter run that replaces it, as a delta against SPIKE-14 so the marker cost is attributable. The *shape* of the answer is robust to a 2–3× error in that coefficient; the exact K and ceiling are not. **No product code changed** — same discipline as SPIKE-A/C/D/H.
**Spike question:** On SPIKE-14's harness and two desktop platforms, over a realistic trip bbox: measure frame time and memory with candidates on screen at SPIKE-A's densities, layered over the basemap, at several zooms while panning/zooming, reported against SPIKE-14's numbers; compare clustering-for-display, zoom thresholds, and salience-gated rendering; test list↔map two-way selection and cluster-extent latency, not just frame rate; include polygon area anchors; re-measure A16 and give the workspace a memory budget with candidates displayed.
**Decides:** The candidate/proposal rendering strategy for the curation workspace (**salience-gated widget markers + canvas-dot tail + clustering backstop**), the display-density ceiling (**~2,800 on GPU**), and the restated A16 (**~1.15 GB, candidate layer immaterial**) — **closing ARCH Q15.**
**Done when:** Frame time, memory and selection latency are quantified with candidates and area anchors on screen at realistic bbox density on two desktop platforms, one strategy is recommended on evidence, and A16 is re-stated — **or the ceiling is documented precisely enough that N3/N4a's map half can be scoped to it. Met — on a calibrated model, with the live harness specified.**

### SPIKE-F — Anonymous web reading surface ✅ **COMPLETE**
**Covers:** PRD **FR132**, **FR116**, **FR138** — story **H13** `[P1]`; ARCH §10.3, §13.4, **Q17**, risk **A26**
**Priority:** Joint **design, security and product** call — not a library choice. Gated to the web/hosted leg (Leg 4); not an MVP blocker; final before any web presentation ships.
**Run:** 2026-08-30 · **Result:** [`spikes/SPIKE-F/results/RESULTS.md`](../spikes/SPIKE-F/results/RESULTS.md) — **Q17's "likely resolution" holds on all three strands, and the two it framed as security questions each get a mechanism rather than a posture.** An anonymous share-token reader is served the **always-visible set only**; revealed content requires an account. Because this is a decision spike, the harness (`spikes/SPIKE-F/`, stdlib only, no `plotlines_core`) exists to make each answer *checkable* — `run_spike.py` fails unless nine verdict clauses hold; they do.
**Token carrier — the one strand that was measurable, and was measured.** A live stdlib server records every request line and header. A share token carried in the **path** (`/read/{token}`) or the **query string** appears in the access log **and** in the `Referer` of every subresource the page pulls — 2/2 requests in each case. A **URL fragment** (`/read#{token}`) keeps the token off the wire entirely (RFC 3986 §3.5) but not out of browser history or a copied link, and it still has to be read by JS and exchanged with the server somehow. **Exchange-for-cookie wins:** `GET /read/{token}` sets an opaque `__Host-pl_read` cookie (`HttpOnly; Secure; SameSite=Strict`, `Max-Age` = token TTL) and 302-redirects to a tokenless `/j/{opaque}`; the token then appears in the log **exactly once** (the initial redirected hit) and the cookie is *not* the token. `Referrer-Policy: no-referrer` on every reading-view response. Same reasoning as ARCH D15, one auth shape down. The §10.3 token contract is unchanged — still revocable (`DELETE /shares/{token}`, checked on every `/j/{opaque}` hit), still scoped to one trip's reveal-filtered content.
**Log retention.** Two tiers. **Edge/CDN access logs** are written before any Plotlines code runs, so the mitigation is the carrier decision above (no token in the URL ⇒ no token in them) plus a short window — **≤72 h, operational only**. **Application request logs** are written through `redact_record`, an **allowlist** (same discipline as `granted_fields`, ARCH §11.1): a request is logged as a route *template* (`/read/{share_token}`), with no token, no `Referer`, no cookie, no raw User-Agent (family only), and the client IP truncated to /24, plus a `retain_until` stamp — **≤30 d**. A field added to the raw record later is dropped by default, so a future log-shape change trips a test rather than leaking. FR138's privacy statement updated to say this in the app's voice, on the reading view itself.
**Reveal for an accountless reader — the "model gap", closed without new machinery.** There is no `reveal_state` row for an anonymous reader and there never will be; rather than invent a home for it, the reader **is the empty revealed set**, permanently. `anonymous_view(payload)` takes **no identity argument** (`inspect` asserts the signature is `(payload)` only) — there is deliberately nowhere to pass "this reader may see more", because that parameter is exactly how the export-corner spoiler (A22) happens. An `on_arrival` plot point renders as a **withheld placeholder** that keeps its `kind` and `arc` stage but drops all content — *not* an omission, because FR116 requires the arc's shape to survive and a hole in the list cannot carry shape (`arc_shape()` over the projection still returns `[exposition, crux, rising_action]`). Hazards (role-level and passage `hazards[]`) and provisions are unconditional. Placeholder over nothing-at-all is a deliberate call: FR116 makes the arc's shape a requirement, and the placeholder doubles as the FR142(e) teaching moment ("this is held for arrival — the product working, not broken").
**Consequences, applied 2026-08-30:** **ARCH D59 added** (token carrier + log tiers + anonymous reveal model); **Q17 closed**; **A26 amended** from "run SPIKE-F" to a concrete mitigation with two build-time residuals named (`share.revoked_at` propagation to minted cookies; CDN access-log retention ≤72 h). **ARCH §10.3 and §13.4** carry the carrier and privacy-statement detail. **PRD FR116** (web-leg reveal), **FR138** (accountless-reader log posture), **story H13's gate** (marked settled), and **two §10 open items** updated. **No product code changed** — the harness is a spike-local prototype and the spec for the Dart `RevealResolver` work when H13 is built, same discipline as SPIKE-A/C/D/G/H.
**Left open, deliberately:** the `/read` endpoint and the Flutter reading view are Leg 4 and unbuilt — this spike settles the decision, not the implementation; `share.revoked_at` → cookie propagation is specified (one indexed lookup per `/j/{opaque}` hit) but not load-tested; the ≤72 h edge-log window is a constraint the hosting choice (Render + custom domain, §10.3) must honour, and if a chosen CDN cannot bound retention that tightly this spike has surfaced a procurement constraint, not a design change; TTS on the reading view (FR40a) reads only what the resolver released, so it inherits the reveal strand unchanged and was not separately exercised.
**Unknown:** FR132 gives Characters a web reading surface with content equivalent to the app; serving it to a reader with **no account** raises three strands PRD §9 states as a gate — a share token in a URL leaking through `Referer`, history and logs; access/CDN logs as a retention surface for people who never consented to an account; and reveal state having nowhere to live for a reader with no account and no GPS. ARCH Q17 names the likely resolution (always-visible set only) and then says it is *"a decision to make, not an assumption to inherit."*
**Spike question:** Settle all three strands and write the resolution into ARCH's Decision Log — the token carrier (fragment vs. path vs. exchange-for-cookie; `Referrer-Policy`; what reaches access logs), log retention (what is logged for an accountless reader, for how long, and whether FR138's statement says so truthfully), and the anonymous-reader reveal model (decide it; if always-visible-set-only, say what sits where an unrevealed plot point would, and confirm FR116 still holds).
**Decides:** ARCH **Q17**, risk **A26**, and what an anonymous reader is actually served.
**Done when:** The token carrier, the log-retention posture, and the anonymous-reader reveal model are decided and recorded as a Decision Log entry, with FR138's privacy statement updated to match — before any web presentation ships. **Met — D59 recorded, Q17 closed, and each decision has a checkable prototype in `spikes/SPIKE-F/`.**

### SPIKE-E — Driving-mode routing and vehicle-access advisory ✅ **COMPLETE**

**Priority:** Implementation-informing — **ran before C13**
**Run:** 2026-08-31 · **Result:** [`spikes/SPIKE-E/results/RESULTS.md`](../spikes/SPIKE-E/results/RESULTS.md) — **the solver passes and the graph configuration fails, and §7.4's own sentence is why it was never noticed.** ARCH called driving *"the cheapest new mode in the list (the OSM road graph is already built; the weights are near-trivial)"* and then said it was unmeasured. The road graph **is** already built — and `multimodal/modes.py` gives driving `network_type="drive"`, which is not a routing choice but an **OSMnx download filter**, and that filter excludes `highway=track` **and** `highway=service` before a single way reaches the graph. Both non-`unclassified` trailhead access roads in this set are exactly those classes: **Gregory Canyon Road is `highway=service`, so the shipped configuration routes to 265 m short of the trailhead and reports success** — the destination snaps to the nearest surviving node and issue #154's 3 km `OutsideGraphExtent` guard is nowhere near tripping, so the Author is handed a cue sheet that stops at the canyon mouth with no indication the road they asked about is not in the map. Within 15 km **driving distance** of the Big Sandy trailhead the shipped filter keeps **32.5 of 76.3 km — 42.6%** of the approach corridor. The offline reconstruction of that filter agrees with a live `network_type="drive"` pull on **99.61% of way ids**, so this is osmnx's behaviour and not a reading of it.
**The engine is not the problem and the weights are decoration.** Cold solves **2.8–14.5 ms** (warm 1.4–3.9 ms) over 722–12,702 edges — driving costs nothing that cycling does not. But `edge_cost` scales every penalty by `1 − directness`, and driving ships `directness=0.95`, so its `quiet`/`scenic`/`surface_paved` are applied at **one twentieth strength**: sweeping `surface_paved` from −1 to +1 leaves the route **byte-identical in three of the four approaches** (in the fourth it moves the paved fraction 31.3% → 35.2%). The dial works at `directness=0.0`; it is switched off by the profile it ships in. **The "flees to the paved network" worry cannot arise where it was aimed** — on a remote approach the route is forced (Big Sandy: 40.3 km gravel of 71.8 km, because there is one road).
**Time is wrong on exactly the leg FR29 was written for.** `base_speed_kmh=60.0` flat gives Big Sandy **71.8 min against 129.6 min** surface-aware from tags already on the edge (**−44.6%**, an hour short), and **+19.7%** on the mostly-paved Middle Fork approach — wrong in both directions, worst on the drive whose length is the reason the requirement exists. FR29's "a real route with distance, **time**, and a cue sheet" is blocked on B7/FR16.
**Two shipped defects the spike walked into.** **(a)** `trips/cues.py:route_polyline` sets `start_index = len(coords)` *before* appending the edge's own points, so every edge after the first reports `start_m` at its **second** vertex and its first sub-segment belongs to no edge: the spans do not tile, leaving **13.9–26.4% of route length attributed to nothing**. `surface_cues` measures its runs against `surface_min_run_m = 150 m` off those spans, so a genuine 160 m run can be silently suppressed and every surface cue lands one sub-segment late. No test asserts the current behaviour; the fix is `start_index = max(0, len(coords) - 1)`. **(b)** `graph/regions.py` never downloads `4wd_only`, `motor_vehicle`, `motorcar` or `ford`, and `barrier` is node-tagged where `useful_tags_node` carries nothing — so **two of FR29a's six signals are unreadable**, driving's `access_key="motor_vehicle"` silently degrades to the generic `access=*` rule, `ford_passable=False` never fires, and `_BARRIER_DEFAULTS` is unreachable **for every mode**, including the gate-across-a-forest-road case §7.4 cites as the reason a driving leg is a route at all. Punch-list §5.3's dependency is a code gap, not only a markdown one.

Per-edge flagging is unreadable — Big Sandy produces **65 raw flagged runs** on one continuous dirt road, because a mapper tags the stretch they drove — so runs merge at the same requirement across **unsurveyed** gaps ≤500 m and never across a stretch tagged clear: **65 → 1 section, 42.6 of 71.8 km, "needs AWD, you declared 2WD"**. **Coverage, measured on approach roads rather than the network:** `4wd_only` is **0 tagged ways in 12,069 ways / 2,419 km** across four regions — FR29a names it first and OSM does not have it. `smoothness` is `absent` at network scale everywhere (0.3–4.3%) and **100% of Big Sandy's last 3.4 km** (31 ways `smoothness=bad`) — §7.4's "strongest single indicator" survives with the denominator corrected. `surface` is the workhorse (79.9 / 24.8 / 22.6 / 33.6% network; 97.0 / 44.8 / 82.6 / 78.5% on-route). **The issue's "the network average will be flattering" premise is right in one region and inverted in another, for the same reason**: Big Sandy's route is *better* surveyed than its network (85.1% vs 62.8% of km) because the people who tag a remote forest road are the people who drove it, while the Middle Fork's last 5 km is **0.6% surveyed by km**.

### SPIKE-19 — Waterway routing on USGS 3DHP, and binding live gauges to reaches ✅ **COMPLETE**
**Run:** 2026-08-16 · **Result:** [`spikes/SPIKE-19/results/RESULTS.md`](../spikes/SPIKE-19/results/RESULTS.md) — **the succession is safe, and one documented design decision is wrong.** All four attributes SPIKE-04's verdict rests on survive: `flowdir`→`flowdirection`, `streamorde`→`streamorder`, `fromnode`/`tonode`→`hydrosequence`/`dnhydrosequence`, and `reachcode` **moved off the flowline** to a hydrolocation layer. **Nothing lost.** A real **148.2 km downstream route on the French Broad** comes out of 3DHP geometry between the same access points SPIKE-04 used (its 151.1 km OSM route, now directed and solved in 0.01 s), and **five live USGS gauges bind to it by identifier** reading 130 → 512 → 840 → 1,240 → 1,370 cfs downstream. Network length lands within **0.1%** (WNC) and **1.6%** (SW WI) of SPIKE-04's NHDPlus HR figures. **Closed.**
**What must change — ARCH §13.2 is wrong as written:** it requires `reachcode` on every `WaterwayGraph` edge, and 3DHP flowlines do not carry one. Measured over **all 112** real-time sites in the three regions rather than a sample: `mainstemid` binds **77.8%** of NLDI-resolved sites, reach code **80.6%**, and **the two together 94.4%**. A related trap is measured and must not be "fixed": the two `mainstemid` namespaces (`geoconnex.us/usgs/…` and `…/ref/…`) are **disjoint registries, not aliases** — 0 of 933 numeric ids appear under both — so normalising the prefix away manufactures false joins.
**The strategic finding: the data has not actually moved yet.** Every flowline in all three regions carries `workunitid = "NHD"` and `featuredate = 2023-09-14` — **3DHP in the product's regions is the converted NHD snapshot, not new elevation-derived hydrography.** Migrating now costs almost nothing and gains almost nothing in data terms; what it buys is a **maintained** product instead of an archived one, which is the whole reason to do it. **Three findings that change how it gets built:** (1) **`featuretypelabel` is anti-correlated with paddleability** — every major river in both riverine regions (French Broad, Wisconsin, Kickapoo, Mississippi, Little Tennessee, Pigeon, Tuckasegee) is ~100% `Waterbody Connector`, and the share climbs monotonically with stream order (12.5% at order 4 → 100% at order 8). (3) **Topology must be built by inverting `dnhydrosequence`**, never by reading `uphydrosequence`, which names only the *main* upstream path and silently drops every tributary at a confluence.
**The elevation decision took itself, and D20 holds unchanged.** 3DHP advertises `hasZ: true` and the geometry carries a third ordinate — which is **0.0 on all 40,938 vertices sampled across all three regions**. There is nothing to weigh against GEDTM30; the Z is a placeholder awaiting the remapping that has not happened. **Re-test when a region's `workunitid` stops reading `NHD`.**
**Also settled:** the **corridor clip** is not a constraint — a true 2 km geodesic buffer around the 176 km run is **392 KB gzipped**, roughly a sixth of the ~2.5 MB of basemap SPIKE-14 prices for the same area, giving FR64 both halves of its budget. The **paddleable-water threshold** (SPIKE-04 outstanding item 2) is taken: **order ≥ 4 as the shipped default**, because order ≥ 3 roughly doubles the network while the longest downstream run barely moves and the largest component's share *falls* in all three regions — the extra water hangs off the network rather than extending it. And the operational story is far better than SPIKE-04 §8's Overpass ordeal: every 3DHP pull ran straight through, no key, no quota, no rate limiting.
**Left open, deliberately:** the provider is a **spike harness, not `plotlines-core` code** — nothing here is written against the real `WaterwayDataProvider` protocol or wired into scoring; **no route was scored** (a shortest path is not a good paddling route); access points are still OSM's, with all of SPIKE-04 §4's problems intact; and SPIKE-04's items **1** (confirm American Whitewater) and **3** (a non-US region) are untouched. A 19.8% network difference in Southern California is reported, not explained.
**Covers:** FR14/FR14a (story **B8**, Leg 3), FR15/B6/H11 (portages and water detail), FR10/FR12 (paddling as a first-class mode, transition nodes); ARCH §6.4 (uncertain multimodal data), §6.5 (nothing blocks a solve), **§11 — both USGS rows**, §13.2 (`WaterwayDataProvider`, `WaterwayGraph.reachcode`), risk **A2**, decision **D19**; MVP §1.2's call that *the paddling graph provider lands in Leg 3 alongside B8*. Picks up SPIKE-04's outstanding items **2** (paddleable-water threshold) and **4** (implement the provider) — not item 1 (American Whitewater) or item 3 (a non-US region), which are a licensing conversation and a separate assessment.
**Priority:** Implementation-informing — it *was* conditionally scope-shaping, and **the condition did not fire**: the attribute check came back clean, so paddling's scope is untouched and what changed is an architectural contract (ARCH §13.2) rather than a PRD story.

**Why this runs before the Leg 3 provider is written: the source SPIKE-04 settled on has been retired.** SPIKE-04 measured **NHDPlus High Resolution** and made it load-bearing — ARCH §11's waterway row, §13.2's `reachcode` rule, and D19's whole scope call sit on it. **USGS retired the NHD on 1 October 2023 and shifted production to the 3D Hydrography Program**; NHDPlus HR remains downloadable but is **no longer maintained**, and 3DHP replaces NHD, WBD and NHDPlus HR with a single product built from lidar-derived elevation. So what this repo currently documents as the paddling network source is a **frozen snapshot**, and 3DHP is not an alternative source to weigh against it — it is its successor. Put that beside the decommissioning of USGS WaterServices in **Q1 2027** that SPIKE-04 §5 already scheduled, and **both halves of the paddling data answer are mid-migration at the same time.** This spike is migration-readiness as much as feature work, which is why it should run before the Leg 3 provider is written rather than after.

**The first task is a regression check, not a feature.** SPIKE-04's network verdict rests on exactly four declared attributes (§3.2), and whether 3DHP carries them — under those names or any names — is unverified. Each failure has a known, specific consequence, which is what makes this cheap to test and expensive to skip:

| SPIKE-04 attribute | What it settled | If 3DHP does not carry it |
|---|---|---|
| `fromnode` / `tonode` | Topology **declared**, not inferred | Connectivity goes back to guessing whether two drawn lines share a vertex — the precise thing §3.2 credits NHD for avoiding |
| `flowdir` | Which way the water goes (100% populated) | The router can send a Character **up** a rapid |
| `streamorde` | Objective "big enough to float a boat" | The Los Angeles flood channels return to the paddling network (§3.1) |
| `reachcode` | The identifier space USGS gauges are indexed in | Gauge association reverts to a spatial nearest-neighbour guess — wrong exactly at confluences and below dams |

The flowline layer's own metadata (`usgs_3dhp_all/FeatureServer/1`) did not return when this entry was written, which is a reason to verify rather than assume.

**And the identifier join is the sharpest unknown in the spike.** It has an obvious wrong answer: bind gauges on whichever identifier the flowline service happens to expose — `NHDPlusID`, `Permanent_Identifier` — and match the remainder by proximity. ARCH §13.2 requires `reachcode` on every edge specifically because **NLDI indexes NWIS sites by it**. 3DHP is organised around *mainstem* identifiers. Which identifier actually joins **3DHP ↔ NLDI ↔ NWIS site** is the one question that, answered wrong, silently turns SPIKE-04's strongest result — 58 of 59 gauges resolved to a reach by lookup — back into a proximity match.

**Boundaries this spike inherits.** A waterway provider touches enough of the system to drift outside its own question, and five of the constraints it must respect are already settled elsewhere. They are listed because each has an appealing shortcut:

- **It runs in the core.** `WaterwayDataProvider` is Python in `plotlines-core`, because a data source has to influence `edge_cost` *during* scoring (ARCH §13.1, P1/D21/FR92). A client that queries USGS directly is a map overlay wearing a provider's name — and it would give sidecar and hosted deployments different water data.
- **It reads a local extract, not a live service.** SPIKE-04 §8 could not complete one region's pull from the public endpoints without tiling each bbox, caching per tile and pacing queries 20 s apart. Same rule as ARCH §14.1's committed fixtures, and the only way to honour §6.5's "nothing blocks a solve".
- **Caching stays on the device.** Per-source adapters inside the provider, with volatility-matched TTLs (P7) — gauge values short, reach linkage long, exactly as ARCH §11 already splits them. A hosted normalization proxy is a **sixth item on P3's list and therefore a design event**; SPIKE-17 is where that evidence gets gathered, and desktop MVP has no hosted tier to put one in.
- **The gauge payload carries a reading, never a verdict.** FR14 as narrowed by D19: current value in the Author's chosen unit (cfs or stage), an age stamp per FR66, and whether it falls inside **the Author's** band. A `status: "runnable"` field is a difficulty grade — the capability D19 removed — and a safety claim no discharge number supports. Class stays Author-declared; American Whitewater's inventory is not licensable and must not be scraped.
- **Rendering and isolates are not this spike's questions.** The flowline overlay is a `flutter_map` polyline layer (D22 — no MapLibre binding runs on Flutter desktop at all), and SPIKE-14 already measured route geometry as **free**: 0% of frames over the 16.7 ms budget at 41k vertices. Where client-side geometry work needs an isolate belongs to **SPIKE-15**, over the whole trip payload. The display surface here is B8's segment inspector on **Author Desktop** — the position-aware cue sheet is FR47 / story I1 in Leg 5, gated by SPIKE-06 and SPIKE-12, and a data-provider spike should not depend on an unbuilt field runtime.

**One decision this spike must take rather than assume: where a river's gradient comes from.** 3DHP is built from lidar-derived elevation and its flowlines are Z-aware, which makes stream slope look free. **D20 names GEDTM30 via OpenTopography as the single elevation source with no fallback**, so drawing slope from flowline Z introduces a second source, and a river's gradient and the trip's elevation profile can then disagree about the same segment. Neither answer is obvious — a lidar-derived water surface may genuinely beat a 30 m DEM on a river. Measure both on a known reach and record the outcome as a decision: either D20 holds and flowline Z is ignored, or water-surface elevation becomes a stated exception in the Decision Log. ✅ **Taken 2026-08-16 — D20 holds, unchanged.** No trade-off existed to weigh: the third ordinate is advertised (`hasZ: true`) and **0.0 on all 40,938 vertices sampled in all three regions**. Ignore flowline Z, and re-test when a region's `workunitid` stops reading `NHD` — that is when the question genuinely reopens.

**Unknown:** Whether the paddling network SPIKE-04 proved exists is still reachable from the product that replaced its source, and whether the gauge→reach join survives the identifier change. Underneath that sit three things SPIKE-04 explicitly did not do: it analysed **attributes, not geometry** (§10 — "building the provider will need the geometry pull this spike skipped"), so no NHD-based route has ever been computed; the **paddleable-water threshold** was left as a product decision measured two ways (order ≥ 4 and ≥ 3, roughly 2× the network) and never taken; and **no route was scored** — a connected directed graph is not a good paddling route.
**Spike question:** For at least two of SPIKE-04's three regions — reuse the same bboxes, because comparability against `spikes/SPIKE-04/raw/` is most of the value — pull real 3DHP flowline **geometry** and answer the four-attribute table above by name. Build a `WaterwayDataProvider` implementation in `plotlines-core` against it, from a **local extract rather than a live service** (SPIKE-04 §8: the public endpoints could not complete that spike's pulls without tiling, caching and pacing), and solve a real put-in→take-out route on it — the geometry-based route SPIKE-04 could not produce. Bind at least one live NWIS gauge to a segment of that route **by identifier, not proximity**, and record which identifier carried the join. Pull both quantities paddlers actually quote — `00060` discharge (cfs) and `00065` gauge height (ft) — from `api.waterdata.usgs.gov` rather than the endpoint being decommissioned, and specify the normalized payload the client receives: site, coordinates, both values, age stamp, and the band comparison, and nothing that grades the water. Confirm nothing in the path blocks a solve (§6.5). Then measure the corridor clip: on-disk size of a buffered flowline network for a multi-day paddle, against SPIKE-14's ~3.5 MB per 1,000 km² of basemap, so FR64's package budget has both halves. Finally, take the threshold decision — stream order, mean annual flow, or both — and re-measure the network against it.
**Decides:** Whether `WaterwayDataProvider` is implemented against **3DHP** or deliberately pinned to the archived NHDPlus HR snapshot with a stated end-of-life; the join identifier that replaces or confirms `reachcode` in ARCH §13.2; whether water-surface Z is an exception to D20; and the paddleable-water threshold that every SPIKE-04 network number moves with. A negative result on the attribute table does not remove paddling — it makes the frozen snapshot the source of record and puts a dated migration risk in the register, which is a **worse** answer than it looks, because the data stops improving while the rivers do not.
**Done when:** A real paddling route is solved from 3DHP geometry with declared topology in at least two regions, a live gauge is bound to one of its segments by identifier, the four-attribute checklist is answered yes/no with the actual field names, the normalized gauge payload is written down as a schema, and the corridor-clip size is quantified — or the regression is documented precisely enough that ARCH §11, §13.2 and D19 can be revisited on evidence. **Met in full, across all three regions rather than two** (routes in two; Southern California produced none, for the same reason SPIKE-04 found — its access points are lake ramps that do not sit on the river network).

### SPIKE-17 — Community data-input extensions over normalized JSON
**Amended 2026-08-28.** PRD/ARCH v2.0 split this spike's scope: the *data-input* half (a `LayerProvider`, FR100, feeding the curation pipeline) moved to **Leg 2.5** and ran as **SPIKE-H (#160)** — see above. What remains here — `EdgeDataProvider`/`NodeDataProvider`/`WaterwayDataProvider` for **routing-graph annotation** (traffic, closures, realtime conditions) and the **output** direction (§14.3) — stays Leg 7, unrun.
**Covers:** FR84 (Leg 7, deliberately open); ARCH §13.1 (two plugin directions), §13.2 (`EdgeDataProvider` / `NodeDataProvider` / `ShapeDataProvider` / `WaterwayDataProvider`), P6 (plugins extend, never modify), P7 (external resources are borrowed), P3 (server-side state is exceptional and enumerable)
**Priority:** Scope-shaping for Leg 7 — **and one of its sub-questions reaches back into MVP principles**
**Unknown:** ARCH §13 defines the *shape* of a data-input plugin and deliberately leaves the contract open, on the reasoning that the built-in OSM path implementing the same interfaces is the proof they are real. That reasoning has never been tested against a **second, differently-shaped source.** The candidate sources are wildly heterogeneous — ArcGIS MapServer REST, OGC API - Features, WaterML, bespoke DOT feeds, vendor APIs with keys and rate limits — and it is unproven that one `annotate_edges` / `fetch_nodes` contract absorbs them, or that a community contributor could write one without touching core code (P6's actual test).

Three specific unknowns sit inside that:

1. **Does the interface hold across source shapes?** SPIKE-04 already produced one hard case — the paddling network needed USGS NHDPlus HR with declared `fromnode`/`tonode` topology and a `reachcode` per edge, not OSM's inferred shared vertices. That is one non-OSM source and it changed the provider's shape. A second and third will say whether §13.2 generalizes or whether it was fitted to two examples.
2. **Edge-annotation sources are the hard half, and the reason is timing.** A node source (NPS sites, historic markers, campgrounds) is a fetch and a map pin. An **edge** source — realtime traffic, construction, closures — must influence `edge_cost` *during scoring*, which means it has to be resolved and cached before the solve, not fetched during it (ARCH §6.5's rule that nothing blocks a solve). Whether a realtime feed can be usefully snapshotted into a graph annotation, and what staleness rule applies (FR66's age-stamping generalized beyond weather), is unaddressed.
3. **Does normalization need a server?** The note proposes a *serverless edge proxy* to flatten USGS WaterML and weather APIs into light JSON. That is convenient and it **conflicts with P3 and P2 as written**: P3 enumerates exactly five things the hosted service does and normalization is not among them, and desktop MVP has no hosted tier at all (MVP §1.2). The honest alternatives are per-source adapter code inside the provider (no server, more client code, key handling on device) or a proxy that is explicitly added to P3's list as a sixth item by decision. **Adding it silently would be the P3 violation the principle exists to catch.**

**Spike question:** Implement two data-input providers against the existing §13.2 interfaces without changing core code, chosen to be maximally unlike each other and unlike OSM — suggest **one node source** and **one edge source** from the candidate list below. Record every place the interface had to bend. Then, for the edge source, measure the fetch-and-annotate step against a real graph build: how long, how cacheable, what TTL the data's actual volatility justifies (P7), and what a stale annotation should surface to the Author. Finally, write down what a third-party contributor would have to know to add a third source — the packaging, the key handling, the registration — since FR84's whole claim is that the interface is *clean*.

## PRD changes these implied

SPIKE-01/02/03 produced two amendments to `Plotlines_PRD.md`. **Both were applied on
2026-08-14**, and are recorded here with the reasoning that produced them.

### 1. Promote A9 (FR8a) from P1 to MVP, capped at two via-nodes ✅ **applied**

A9's priority note already specifies the condition: *"if via-node support is the natural
way the router implements start/destination handling (i.e. a loop's start and a
via-node are the same constraint mechanism), it should land in MVP rather than being
artificially deferred."* SPIKE-01 confirmed that literally — one `solve_circuit` call
serves every route shape, and a 1-via loop is **6× faster** than an unconstrained one
because the via replaces an anchor the engine would otherwise have to search for.

The cap is the new information: beyond two vias the distance envelope cannot be
honoured (+30.7% to +81.9% error), so a **3+ via-node feature stays P1** and needs
A6's relaxation UI to go with it.

**Applied:** the story was **split in two** so the MVP half can be built and closed
without carrying an open caveat. **A9 — Route a loop through one or two designated
nodes** `[MVP]`, whose AC now also requires a genuine loop with retraced road reported,
and requires A6 to name the via-node rather than the terrain on infeasibility.
**A9a — Route a loop through three or more designated nodes** `[P1]`, a new backlog
story whose AC makes target distance explicitly *advisory*, surfaces the deviation, and
offers A6's relaxation path in the same interaction. A9a is a UI-and-expectations
story, not a solver one: at three vias every via was still hit and every loop still
closed — only the distance envelope broke. FR8a's row names both stories; the MVP scope
doc's routing row and the 95 → 96 story count were updated to match.

### 2. Resolve the FR6 / A5 disagreement about what a band bounds ✅ **applied**

FR6: *"Authors set a **min and max** on any **weight**."*
A5 AC: *"engine returns a route **within all bands** where one exists; where none
exists, A6 governs."*

These cannot both be true. A band on a *weight value* is never infeasible — every
number inside it is a legal weight — so A5's "where none exists" clause becomes
unreachable and A6's conflict path becomes dead code for FR6. A band on a *realised
attribute* ("between 400 and 600 m of climbing") can genuinely be infeasible, which is
what makes A5's AC and A6 mean something, and is what SPIKE-02 and SPIKE-03 both
implement and measure against.

**Decision: FR6 reworded to bound the realised attribute**, keeping A5. This was the
smaller edit and preserved the story that already depends on it; the alternative —
softening A5's AC to match FR6's literal wording — would have removed the only route by
which A6 ever fires from a weight band.

**Applied:** FR6 now reads as a band on the realized value of a weighted route
attribute, explicitly "not the weight setting". A5's AC gained two clauses the spike
earned: band controls open on the region's attainable envelope rather than a fixed
absolute scale, and band precision is floored in absolute units so a control cannot ask
for a resolution the terrain cannot support.

### Lower-priority, implementation-level (no PRD wording change required)

- **FR4's surface weight needs to be bipolar** (−1 avoid … 0 indifferent … +1 seek),
  matching FR2's `peaks`. As specified it can only *tolerate* unpaved surfaces, never
  prefer them, so "relative preference across paved / gravel / singletrack" is not
  currently expressible. No FR text change needed — the requirement is already written
  as a *relative preference*; the implementation simply has to match it.
- **Distance should be banded by default**, not treated as a soft target (FR8), or the
  compromise quietly spends the Author's mileage (+14.8% measured).
